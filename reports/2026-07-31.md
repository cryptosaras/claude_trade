### Item 2 — regime occupancy over 30 days: the session's main measurement

No prior report states what fraction of tape each regime label actually occupies.
Every strategy in the book declares a `regimes` list, and `core.step` treats that
list as a hard on/off switch — so **occupancy is a hard multiplier on every
strategy's achievable sample rate, independent of whether it has any edge.**

Measurement: from the `regime` table, the **duration-weighted** share of the last
30 days spent in each label (not the row share — if rows are written per tick
rather than per change, a row count would silently answer a different question).
Also: per-label occupancy over the book's whole live history, and the count and
median length of regime episodes.

**Branches, fixed now:**

- **R1 — SIDE ≥ 50% of tape.** Then the book has a **structural mismatch**: the
  book's declared regimes are concentrated where the tape rarely sits, and low
  trade counts across the book are partly an artifact of declaration, not of
  absent edge. Named as a design constraint for the next session.
- **R2 — SIDE between 35% and 50%.** Mismatch is present but not dominant;
  recorded as a secondary factor, no strong claim.
- **R3 — SIDE < 35%.** The mismatch framing is **dropped entirely** and this
  session says so plainly.

**Pre-committed regardless of branch: this measurement CANNOT change any
strategy's `meta.regimes` today, and no strategy file is edited on the strength
of it.** Occupancy says how *often* a gate is open. It says nothing about whether
the edge behind the gate is real. Using it to widen regimes would be trading more
in tape a strategy was never shown to work in — a sample-count argument wearing
an edge argument's clothes. It is an **input to the next session's design**, and
the deliverable is the number plus its consequence, stated.

### Item 3 — the 62.4% stop-out, in its non-dial form

Watchlist item 2 from Jul 30 (the 332 stop-losses at −33,940 against 160
take-profits at +30,546) is the largest unexplained structure in the live record.
Jul 30 also correctly warned it is adjacent to the stop-distance work that Jul 28
and Jul 29 both closed as a dead end.

**So it is explicitly NOT approached as a parameter.** No `min_sl_pct`,
`max_sl_pct`, `sl_atr` or `tp_r` is touched, and **no backtest is run for this
item.** Instead, decompose the 532-trade live census **per strategy** into: win
rate, average win, average loss, realized reward:risk, and — the point of the
exercise — **the win rate that strategy would need to break even at its own
realized R:R**. Comparing required against actual converts "62% of trades stop
out" from a description into a stated requirement, and tests a specific
explanation: **that the book's deficit is win rate, not stop placement.** If so,
it explains *why* three sessions of stop-distance tuning found nothing — they
were adjusting the denominator of the wrong ratio.

Pooled arithmetic from Jul 30's own census predicts the answer: 32.5% actual win
rate against ~35.5% required at 1.87 R:R. Writing that prediction down now so the
per-strategy table cannot be read as a discovery if it merely reproduces it.

**Constraints:** per-strategy rows are reported for **n ≥ 20 only**; below that
the row is shown but carries **no claim**. This is arithmetic on closed live
trades — no backtest, so no window drift, no slot-reshuffle confound, and no
tuning-window contamination. It also cannot be causal: R:R is realized, not
counterfactual, and a strategy that changed its stops mid-life pools two regimes
of behaviour.
