### Why this is a census, not a backtest

My first design was a `whale_absorb_hold` strategy file with a 50%-away stop, run
through the backtester against a `whale_absorb` control. **I am discarding that
design before running it, and recording why**, because the flaw is instructive:

- Every hold would run the full 8h, while the control exits at 1.5R in ~5h.
  With `max_positions_per_strategy: 3` and `max_positions_per_group: 2`, the hold
  variant would **decline entries the control accepted** — and the declines would
  not be random. They would fall on exactly the bursty absorption cascades this
  repo has documented (ORDI×2, TRB×5, JASMY×4). "Hold vs control" would partly be
  measuring *which trades each was allowed to take*, which is the slot confound
  already recorded in this repo's own notes.
- A 50% stop distance also drives `notional = risk/sl_dist` to ~123 USDT, a
  40× different sizing regime from the control.

**The census has neither problem.** It computes the forward 8h return from every
qualifying signal directly — no broker, no slots, no sizing, no leverage. That is
how the study itself measured, so it is a like-for-like replication rather than a
different experiment wearing the same name.

### What is measured

Rebuild the clause census (yesterday's throwaway tool; the "Note on method"
flagged it as worth rebuilding — this is the third session to need it). For every
closed 15m bar in the window, on every symbol, evaluate `whale_absorb`'s entry
conjunction **exactly as `should_enter` does** — `vol ≥ 2.5× avg20`,
`range ≤ 0.9 ATR`, `low within 0.3 ATR of the 12h low`, `6h return ≤ −1.5%`, and
the resulting 2.5-ATR stop distance inside [0.5%, 5%]. Then, from each signal,
measure three variants **on the identical signal set**:

| variant | exit rule |
|---|---|
| **RAW** | hold 32 bars (8h), no stop, no target — *the study's claim* |
| **STOP** | 2.5-ATR stop below the absorption low, else 8h |
| **STOP+TP** | 2.5-ATR stop, 1.5R target, else 8h — *the deployed `whale_absorb`* |

All three net of **0.16%** (0.10% fees round-trip at `fee_taker` 0.0005/side plus
0.06% slippage round-trip at 0.0003/side), which is the same 0.16% the study
states. **No fee or slippage number is touched** — these are read from
`config/settings.yaml`, not chosen.

Entry price is the **close of the signal bar**; the live engine enters at the next
tick, a uniform +15 min later. Stated, not hidden: the census is 15 minutes early
relative to live, on a 32-bar horizon.

## Finding 1 — the replication, three exit variants on one signal set

Window **Jul 6 00:00Z → Aug 1 21:00Z** (26.875 days), pinned before looking, and
fully disjoint from the study's ~Jun 6 → Jul 6. **338,015 closed 15m bars** on
**132 symbols** with ≥90% 1m coverage (of 202 collected). Every variant below is
measured on the **identical 285 signals** — no broker, no slots, no sizing — and
is net of **0.16%** (0.10% fees + 0.06% slippage, read from `settings.yaml`).

**All collected symbols (n=285):**

| variant | mean %/trade | win% | PF | avg win | avg loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RAW** (8h hold, no stop/target) | **−0.356** | 45.96 | **0.732** | +2.122 | −2.465 |
| **STOP** (2.5 ATR, else 8h) | −0.044 | 38.95 | 0.954 | +2.336 | −1.563 |
| **STOP+TP** (2.5 ATR + 1.5R = *deployed*) | −0.061 | 42.46 | 0.933 | +2.008 | −1.587 |

**Group symbols only (n=172):** RAW −0.598 (PF 0.523) / STOP −0.368 (0.617) /
STOP+TP −0.276 (0.698).

**The deployed subset — group symbols, alt groups, BULL+BEAR (n=56):**
RAW −0.464 (PF 0.571) / STOP −0.243 (0.706) / **STOP+TP −0.081 (PF 0.894)**.

Head to head against what the study claimed:

| | study (Jun 6 → Jul 6) | census (Jul 6 → Aug 1) |
|---|---|---|
| RAW 8h hold | **+1.02%**, PF 3.09, n=175 | **−0.356%**, PF 0.732, n=285 |
| deployed config | **+0.31%**, PF 1.36, n=108 | **−0.081%**, PF 0.894, n=56 |

**How much of this is noise?** Taking σ ≈ 3% per trade (the scale of the average
win and loss; the two-point lower bound is 2.29%), SE ≈ 0.18% at n=285. So
**+1.02% is excluded at roughly 7–10 standard errors** — that part is not a
sample-size argument. **Whether the true value is zero or slightly negative is
NOT resolved**: −0.356% sits about 2 SE below zero, and this repo's own power
note puts the "covers costs vs no edge" question at 266–897 trades. The honest
claim is **"the claimed edge is absent", not "the edge is negative".**

**Concentration**, per the standing rule: 285 signals over **98 distinct
symbols**; the top 10 hold **25.6%** and the largest single symbol (`RE_USDT`,
13) is **4.6%**. The negative result is diffuse, not one coin.

**A limitation I am stating rather than glossing.** The census fires **0.087
conjunctions per symbol-day** against the study's implied **0.056** — about
**1.5× more often**. My reconstruction is therefore *not* byte-equal to the
original event definition (which no longer exists to compare against). What
licenses the census as a measurement of **the live engine** is the 7-of-9 entry
reproduction below, not agreement with the vanished study code.

## Finding 2 — the stop is protective, which inverts the study's mechanism

The study's stated mechanism: *"a tight SL below the absorption low destroys it
(PF 0.58 — chop consumes it before the bounce); only a WIDE stop survives."* The
implication I pre-registered from it was that the stop is a cost paid to make the
signal tradeable.

On post-study tape it is the opposite — adding the 2.5-ATR stop **improves**
mean return in every universe:

| universe | RAW | → STOP+TP | improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| all collected (285) | −0.356% | −0.061% | **+0.295 pp** |
| group symbols (172) | −0.598% | −0.276% | **+0.322 pp** |
| deployed subset (56) | −0.464% | −0.081% | **+0.384 pp** |

Three universes, same direction. The mechanism reads plainly in the win/loss
split: the stop cuts the average loss from **−2.465% to −1.587%** while the
average win only falls from **+2.122% to +2.008%**. **There is no bounce to
protect** — after these events price keeps going down, and truncating that is
worth more than the winners it clips.

This also settles yesterday's open question in the opposite direction to my
reasoning. Yesterday `whale_absorb_runner` (stop kept, TP clip removed) was worse
than the control, and I concluded the bounce "reverses" rather than running to
6R. The census says something stronger and simpler: **the 8h forward drift after
these events is negative, so every mechanism that keeps the trade alive longer
loses money.** `whale_absorb`'s remaining PF comes from cutting trades short, not
from an edge in the event.

## Finding 4 — a collector gap can manufacture this strategy's signal

The census matched 7 of 9 live entries. The 2 misses were probed directly, with a
known-good entry as control:

| live entry | vol | **range/ATR (limit 0.9)** | low-prox | 6h drop | SL dist |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ORDI` Jul 28 17:30 *(control, matched)* | 2.85× ✓ | **0.777 ✓** | ✓ | −2.23% ✓ | 1.63% ✓ |
| `UNI` Jul 27 21:30 *(missed)* | 3.61× ✓ | **1.423 ✗** | ✓ | −1.90% ✓ | 1.43% ✓ |
| `JTO` Jul 29 03:30 *(missed)* | 3.37× ✓ | **0.911 ✗** | ✓ | −1.91% ✓ | 1.42% ✓ |

**Both misses fail on exactly one clause, and it is the same clause: the bar is
wider than 0.9 ATR in today's data than it was when the engine traded it.** JTO
misses by 0.011 ATR (1.2% of the threshold) — within ATR-warmup sensitivity and
unremarkable. **UNI at 1.423 is not**: the engine would have needed an ATR 58%
larger, which warmup cannot produce.

**Leading hypothesis, explicitly unverified:** the engine saw an *incomplete* 15m
bar. A missing 1m candle inside 21:15–21:30 makes the resampled bar's high/low
narrower, the collector backfills the minute later, and today the bar is wider.
The direction fits exactly — and the consequence is uncomfortable, because
**`whale_absorb`'s core clause is "huge volume, no price progress". A data gap
removes price progress without removing volume. It manufactures the signal.**

**This is not verifiable from the DB**: `candles` has primary key
`(symbol, tf, ts)` and no insert timestamp, so there is no record of what was
present when. Stated as a candidate, put on the watchlist, not acted on. Any fix
(a bar-completeness guard in the strategy or the engine) is a code change that
needs its own pre-registration.
