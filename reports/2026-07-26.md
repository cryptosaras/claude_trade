Consequence for future sessions: **do not plan a long out-of-sample test.** With
~50 days of 1m data, of which Jun 25 → Jul 26 is already consumed by tuning, the

parameter set, and **not** enough to *validate a new strategy* to the gate's

data-availability fact, not a policy preference — and it is the honest reason
not to author a new strategy off a sweep right now.

## Finding 2 — funding lookahead is immaterial (retires the Jul 16 item)

which is technically lookahead. Magnitude, over all 524 real closed trades:

|---|---|
| avg hold | **1.50h** (median **0.54h**) |

### This does NOT rescue v3, and the held-out window is not why

I set this session up believing the binding problem was *in-sample
contamination*, and that a clean held-out window would therefore be decisive. **It
isn't, and I was wrong about the diagnosis.** Cross-referencing Jul 23:

| window | tuning status | backtest PF | live PF over same tape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 9–25 (this run) | **clean held-out** | **1.29** | — (no live) |
| Jul 16–23 | in-sample | 1.39 | — |
| Jul 21–23 | post-pause | 1.52 | — |
| Jul 16–21 (backed out) | in-sample | ~1.4 | **0.50** |

The backtester prints **1.29–1.52 on every window ever tried**, including the
slice where live simultaneously bled at PF 0.50. A gauge that reads the same
whether live works or fails has **no discriminating power** — the Jul 23 report
established this and my clean window does not change it. Running unbiased data
through a biased instrument yields a biased number.

**So the real finding is a re-diagnosis:** the bottleneck was never window
contamination (which I fixed today, for free, permanently). It is that **the
backtester is uncalibrated against live.** Held-out data cannot help until that
is fixed. v3 stays `paused`; PF 1.29 is not evidence for resuming it, and per
`backtest-vs-live-slot-confound` the backtester is *less* trustworthy than live,
not a court of appeal against it.
