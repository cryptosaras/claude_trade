### The mechanism being measured (restated, because the design depends on it)

`_closed_bars` ([whale_absorb.py:60](strategies/whale_absorb.py:60)) drops the
newest resampled bar only when `df.index[-1] + 15min > ctx.now`. It tests **when
the bar's window ended**, never **whether its 15 minutes of data arrived**. The
collector refreshes each symbol about every 87 s; the engine ticks every 20 s and
was measured entering within 0.00–0.67 min of the bar boundary. So at the tick
where bar `T` first counts as closed, bar `T` is typically missing its **last 1–2
minutes** while every earlier bar is long since complete.

That asymmetry is what makes the measurement tractable: **only the signal bar is
truncated.** Prior bars — which supply `avg20` volume, the 12 h low, the 6 h
reference close, and the ATR history — are complete.

### What is measured — the wedge

For truncation depth `m` (the signal bar missing its last `m` minutes), rebuild
bar `i` from its first `15 − m` 1m candles, leave all prior bars complete, and
re-evaluate `whale_absorb`'s entry conjunction exactly as `should_enter` does.

**ATR is recomputed on the truncated series, not carried over from complete
data.** This is the exact error that forced the Aug 3 correction, and it is
carried in as a standing check. `ind.atr` is a Wilder EMA, so with only bar `i`
modified the correct value is `ATR_i(m) = ATR_{i−1}·(13/14) + TR_i(m)/14`, with
`TR_i(m)` built from the truncated high/low against the **complete** prior close.

**Depths, pre-declared:**

- **`m = 0`** — the specified strategy (complete bars). The baseline.
- **`m = 1` and `m = 2`** — the primary cells, matching the 1.2–1.7 min measured
  staleness and the `sl`-channel bounds established Aug 3 (`ORDI` 527 exactly 1
  missing minute, `JTO` 1–2, `UNI` 1–5).
- **union over `m = 1..5`** — the upper bound. `_closed_bars` re-admits bar `T`
  on every 20 s tick for the following 15 minutes, so a signal reachable at *any*
  tick before the bar completes is reachable by the engine. This is a ceiling,
  not an estimate, and is labelled as one.

**Three classes are counted, not one number:**

| class | meaning |
|---|---|
| **TRUNC-ONLY** | fires under truncation, not on the complete bar — *the engine takes a trade its own rules reject* |
| **BOTH** | fires either way |
| **COMPLETE-ONLY** | fires on the complete bar, not under truncation — *the engine misses a trade its rules require* |

**The wedge is the difference in mean net %/trade between the engine's realized
set and the specified set**, and it is reported **decomposed into two channels**,
because conflating them would be uninterpretable:

- **W1 — signal-set effect only.** Both sets priced at the complete-bar close.
  Isolates *which trades are taken*.
- **W2 — full engine view.** The truncated set priced at the truncated close (the
  engine's `ctx.price`), with its own truncation-derived SL and TP; the specified
  set priced at the complete close. This is what actually happens.

Forward paths start at the bar boundary `T+15` in both cases, on 1m candles.
Three exit variants on each identical signal set, as Aug 2: **RAW** (8 h hold),
**STOP** (2.5 ATR), **STOP+TP** (2.5 ATR + 1.5 R — *the deployed config*). All
net of **0.16%** read from `config/settings.yaml`. **No fee or slippage number is
touched.**

## Finding 2 — clause attribution: two different clauses, pulling opposite ways

The m=0 vs m=1 disagreements decompose cleanly, and the decomposition is the
mechanism:

| direction | n | clauses responsible |
|---|---|---|
| **COMPLETE-ONLY** (fires complete, not truncated) | 42 | **vol 33**, drop 8, low 3 |
| **TRUNC-ONLY** (fires truncated, not complete) | 30 | **rng 21**, drop 9, dist 1 |

Dropping a bar's last minutes can only **narrow** it, which makes
`range ≤ 0.9 ATR` easier — that is the manufacturing channel Aug 2 and Aug 3
identified, and it is confirmed here at n=21 rather than at n=2 live entries. But
it also **removes volume**, which makes `vol ≥ 2.5 × avg20` harder, and that
channel is larger (33 vs 21). **My prediction 1 assumed only the first channel
mattered. It was the smaller one.**

This is why the naive m=1 substitution reading looked so dramatic and was wrong:
`COMPLETE-ONLY` is overwhelmingly *"the volume surge arrived in the bar's final
minute"*, and those signals performed terribly (−0.839%, n=42). That is an
interesting fact about **late-bar volume spikes as absorption signals**, and it
is a candidate for a future session — but it is **not** a wedge component,
because the engine takes those trades a minute later.

## Finding 3 — the design ranking, with the numbers that decide it

**Entry lag is free.** The specified signal set, entered k minutes past the bar
boundary (paired — same signals, same stop, TP recomputed from the later entry):

| lag | all collected (n=298) | group symbols (n=178) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 min | −0.045% | −0.279% |
| 1 min | **+0.006%** | **−0.242%** |
| 2 min | −0.042% | −0.239% |
| 3 min | −0.046% | −0.247% |
| 5 min | −0.078% | −0.304% |

Nothing here is significant either — but the direction matters for the decision:
**waiting 1–3 minutes does not cost money**, and only at 5 minutes does it start
to. This is the first hard evidence about the cost of the lag that both fix
designs (a) and (b) must pay.

**The ranking that follows:**

1. **(a) Require 15 constituent 1m candles before treating a 15m bar as
   closed. — Recommended.** It is deterministic, it removes the entire 21–46%
   `EXTRA` set, and the wait it imposes *is* the collector staleness (1.6–2.0 min
   mean), which the table above prices at ~zero. Its stated drawback on Aug 3 —
   *"it will reduce signal count and change every backtest comparison
   baseline"* — is now quantified: the count falls by 18–31% of the realized set,
   and the baseline moves by **0.01–0.05 pp**, which is not a meaningful
   re-baselining.
2. **(b) Offset the decision tick past the collector's refresh. — Not
   recommended, and the measurement is why.** At the nominal ~90 s it does not
   fix the defect: staleness p90 is such that at T+16.5 min the newest 1m candle
   is still around T+14, leaving the bar a minute short. To be safe it needs a
   3–4 minute offset, i.e. **a longer wait than (a) for a probabilistic guarantee
   instead of a deterministic one.** It is strictly dominated.
3. **(c) Cut the collector cycle so refresh < tick. — Rank last.** Measured
   per-symbol refresh over the last 2 hours: **mean 85.3 s, median 82.0, p90
   102.0, max 128.1** (n=83 gaps, from the collector's own `BTC_USDT` Min1
   request log). ***This figure is a single log scrape of a live process and
   cannot be twinned*** — it is used here only to say the collector is *not
   improving*, which is the weakest claim the ranking needs, and (c) would rank
   last on the "probabilistic, never eliminates truncation" argument alone.
   Essentially unchanged from Aug 3's derived ~87 s, so the
   component is stable but **not improving**, and it is throwing five `510`s in
   ten days. Asking it to run faster raises rate-limit risk and still only makes
   truncation less likely, never impossible.

**None of the three is executed.** The fix is an engine change, this is an
unattended run, and Aug 3 pre-committed that it needs its own pre-registration.
Item 1 of the watchlist is now that pre-registration, and it has a measurement
behind it that it did not have yesterday.

## Note on method

The pre-registration did its job in the way that matters: it stopped me publishing
the wrong headline. The `COMPLETE-ONLY = −0.839%` cell was sitting in the first
census output, it was the most striking number of the day, and the only reason it
is in the *rejected* section rather than the summary is that the void condition
forced me to check the engine's actual entry behaviour — which meant reading
`core.py` — which showed those trades are not missed at all.

That is the same shape as Aug 3's lesson (*one recovered channel is a hypothesis,
not a measurement*) applied one level up: **a model of a system is a hypothesis
until you have read the code that implements it.** I pre-registered a
substitutive model of a defect in an engine whose entry loop I had not read. The
question survived; the operationalization did not. Three sessions in a row now.

The concrete fix for next time is cheap and I am writing it down as a rule rather
than a resolution: **between "what am I asking" and "what do I predict", insert
"what does the code do".** Aug 2 would have caught the slot confound with it.
Aug 3 would have caught the saturated variable with it. Today it would have
produced the additive model in the pre-registration instead of in the correction.
