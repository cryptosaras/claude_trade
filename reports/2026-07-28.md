### Addition: gross PF alone would misclassify the high-turnover strategies

`gross_pnl` is computed from the filled entry/exit prices, so **slippage
(0.03%/side) is already inside it**; only fees are outside. Gross PF therefore
charges each strategy 0.06% of notional per round trip, in proportion to how
often it trades. Four strategies in this book turn over very fast —
`dispersion_fade` (n=517 on window A, 0.4h holds), `breakout_momentum` (378,
0.5h), `vwap_revert_short_capped` (366, 0.8h), `pump_fade` (144). For those, a
gross PF near 1.0 is **not** proof of "no signal to rescue"; it is equally
consistent with a real signal buried under slippage from over-trading — the
exact category watchlist item 2 wants to surface. Publishing a two-bucket table
off gross PF alone would hand the next session a wrong verdict on precisely the
strategies most likely to respond to cost-aware re-tuning.

So the classification is **three-way**, using a per-trade cost decomposition
derived from `config/settings.yaml` (`fee_taker` 0.0005, `slippage` 0.0003 — both
untouched, per the honesty rules):

- fees round trip = `0.001 × notional` ⇒ **`avg notional ≈ fees_per_trade × 1000`**
- slippage round trip = `0.0006 × notional` ⇒ **`slip/trade ≈ 0.6 × fees_per_trade`**
- **`raw edge/trade = gross_edge_per_trade + 0.6 × fees_per_trade`** (signal before *any* cost)
- **total cost/trade = `1.6 × fees_per_trade`**

| bucket | test | meaning | re-tuning prospect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — no raw edge** | raw edge/trade ≤ 0 | directionally wrong before any cost | none; retirement final |
| **B — raw edge, killed by turnover** | raw edge > 0, gross PF ≈ 1.0, short holds / high n | signal exists, slippage eats it | trade less, be more selective |
| **C — raw edge, killed by fees** | gross PF > 1.05, net PF < gross | signal survives slippage, fees finish it | size differently (wider stops → smaller notional) |

Note ahead of the results: `range_fader` (n=108, 2.7h holds) and `sweep_reclaim`
(n=35, 1.7h) are **not** high-turnover, so whatever this screen says, it does not
reverse the Jul 27 vindication of those two retirements. This is a refinement of
that finding, not a contradiction of it.

## PRE-REGISTRATION ADDENDUM — one derived-parameter test on `pdhl_sweep`

Also written before the run. Added mid-session because the cost algebra below
(Finding 1) produced a **falsifiable prediction with no free parameters**, which
is worth more than the screen it came out of.

### The prediction

`pdhl_sweep` sizes at 1% risk over its stop distance and exits at `tp_r = 1.5`.
So for a trade whose stop sits `d` (as a fraction of price) away, the winner is
`+1.5d` and the loser `−d`, and at win rate `p` its edge yield is
**`d · (2.5p − 1)`** against a hard round-trip cost of **0.16% of notional**.
Solving for the stop distance at which a trade can clear its own costs:

| win rate `p` | break-even stop distance |
|---|---|
| 0.48 | 0.800% |
| 0.50 | 0.640% |
| **0.508** (its window-B rate) | **0.593%** |
| 0.52 | 0.533% |
| 0.55 | 0.427% |

**Its `min_sl_pct` floor is 0.400%.** At its own observed win rate, *every trade
it takes with a stop between 0.40% and 0.59% is structurally unprofitable* —
not because the signal is wrong, but because the geometry cannot pay the toll.
The docstring shows the author saw the mechanism (*"skip if stop closer than
0.4% (fees dominate R)"*) and set the floor roughly a third too low.

**Pre-registered prediction:** raising `min_sl_pct` from `0.004` to `0.006`
raises window-B net PF above the current 1.07.

### CORRECTION to this pre-registration, made before the run — the model is optimistic and a null has THREE readings, not one

I wrote that a null would mean "win rate depends on stop distance". That
enumeration was incomplete, and the omitted branch is the more likely one.

The model assumes every winner returns `+1.5d`. But `win_rate` in the
backtester is `net_pnl > 0` (`app/backtest/main.py:175`) — **net**, i.e. merely
having cleared costs. Time-stop and discretionary exits count as wins while
returning far less than `1.5d`. So the true mean `r` is **below** `d(2.5p−1)`,
and the real break-even stop distance is **above** 0.593%. `min_sl_pct = 0.006`
may simply be too low. Book-wide the approximation looks tolerable (TP share
30.3% against a ~31.6% win rate), but `pdhl_sweep`'s window-B win rate is
50.8% — far above book — and no exit-reason split existed for it.

So the driver was rewritten before running to dump, per trade: exit-reason mix,
and mean realized `r` separately for winners and losers. That makes a null
**diagnosable** instead of ambiguous. The three readings, fixed now:

1. PF rises → prediction confirmed.
2. PF flat/down **and** the measured winner payoff is well under `1.5d` → the
   payoff model was optimistic, the corrected break-even is higher, and the
   right response is to pre-register *that* threshold for next session.
3. PF flat/down **and** the payoff model checks out → win rate genuinely depends
   on stop distance, which is the original reading.

### Second correction: this is a comparison of two trade sets, not a filtered subset

The engine shares 6–8 position slots. Raising the floor frees slots that later
signals then occupy, so the variant's trades are **not** a subset of the
baseline's — new trades appear that never had a chance at 0.004. This is the
slot-reshuffle confound already recorded in my notes as having corrupted earlier
param sweeps. The test is still the right one, but **"PF rose" and "the filter
caused it" are separable claims and only the first is directly observed.**
Diagnostic: if `n` stays near 59 while the floor rises 50%, that is slot
backfill rather than a weak filter. The variant's `n` and realized stop-distance
distribution are reported alongside PF for exactly this reason.

### Why this is not tuning on my only clean window

The value **0.006 is derived, not searched**: it is `0.0016/(2.5p−1)` at the
observed `p`, rounded up. There is exactly one candidate and no selection over
window B. That is categorically different from the group-narrowing I refused
last session (which would have picked the winner *out of* window B results).

Honest limits, fixed now:
- I will also run `0.008` **as information only**. It is *not* a decision input
  and no promotion may cite it. Reporting two values is mild selection and I am
  naming it rather than hiding it.
- **No status changes regardless of outcome.** `pdhl_sweep` stays `retired`.

## Finding 1 — THE HEADLINE: there is exactly one number that decides whether a strategy can pay its costs, and it is scale-free

This session set out to use gross PF to classify the book, discovered gross PF
is the wrong instrument, and derived the right one. **This supersedes the
two-bucket/three-bucket framing in my own pre-registration above** — reported as
a correction rather than quietly substituted.

### The algebra, verified against `app/engine/account.py` rather than assumed

`Broker.open` fills at `price·(1+s)` for a long, `Broker.close` at
`price·(1−s)`, with `s = slippage = 0.0003`; fees are `notional · 0.0005` on
each leg. So for a trade with entry notional `N` and underlying price move `r`
(the raw signal, before any cost):

```
gross ≈ N·(r − 0.0006)          slippage is INSIDE gross, both legs
net   ≈ N·(r − 0.0006 − 0.0010) = N·(r − 0.0016)
```

Summing over trades, the book is net-profitable **iff**

> **the notional-weighted mean raw move `r` exceeds 0.16%.**

Call that weighted mean the **edge yield**. It is recoverable from the
backtester's existing output, since `fees_per_trade = 0.001 × notional`:

```
edge yield = (gross_edge_per_trade + 0.6 × fees_per_trade) / (1000 × fees_per_trade)
```

**Trade count, notional, and leverage all cancel** — they scale edge and cost by
exactly the same factor. Funding is immaterial and favourable (+13.43 collected
across 526 trades ≈ 0.0002% of notional) and is excluded.

### Two claims, and only one of them is a theorem — keep them apart

- **Proved:** `Σ Nᵢ(rᵢ − 0.0016) > 0 ⟺ edge yield > 0.16%`, so **× hurdle > 1 is
  exactly equivalent to net PF > 1**. The threshold crossing is algebra, not
  observation, and it is why the sign flips precisely at 1.0× in the table below.
- **Observed only:** that a *higher* edge yield implies a *higher* net PF. Net PF
  is a ratio of positive-sum to negative-sum PnL, and nothing in the algebra
  makes it a monotone function of a notional-weighted mean. The ordering below
  is an empirical regularity at n=6, not a prediction of the framework.

**Rank candidates by edge yield if you like, but do not expect PF to follow it.**
Use it to answer "can this clear costs at all", which it answers exactly.

**The screen produced a counterexample within the hour, which is why this
distinction is in the report rather than a footnote.** On window B,
`ema_trend_rider` has the *higher* edge yield (0.0185% vs 0.0114%) but the
*lower* net PF (0.59 vs 0.67) against `vwap_pullback_long` — a clean inversion.
Final tally across all live and backtest observations this session:

- **threshold rule (× hurdle > 1 ⟺ net PF > 1): 17 of 17 correct, no exceptions**
  — and Finding 4's two failed variants make it **20 of 20**
- **ordering: 2 inversions in 17** (the second: `sweep_reclaim` at −0.38× makes
  PF 0.62, *above* `ema_trend_rider`'s 0.59 at a *higher* +0.12×)

Had I published the ordering as part of the framework, this session's own data
would have falsified it.

### Three corrections this forces — two of them to my own prior claims

1. **My pre-registration's "bucket B" is incoherent and I am striking it.** I
   wrote that high-turnover strategies might have raw edge "killed by turnover",
   fixable by trading less. Wrong: slippage is proportional to notional *per
   round trip*, exactly like fees, so cutting frequency scales edge and cost
   identically and **leaves edge yield unchanged**. Trading less often is not a
   lever at all. Only *selectivity* helps, and only insofar as the trades it
   drops had below-average `r`. The concern that gross PF would misclassify the
   fast strategies was correct; the fix is a better statistic, not a third
   bucket.

3. **Jul 27's mechanism for why `whale_absorb` beats `pdhl_sweep` is wrong.**
   That report said its wide stops mean "smaller notional at fixed 1% risk — so
   it pays less than half the fee load **for the same signal**". Their gross
   dollars per trade *are* near-identical (16.87 vs 17.24), which is what
   invited the reading. But per unit notional the signals are not the same:
   **0.343% vs 0.190%**. `whale_absorb`'s signal is 1.8× stronger; the matching
   dollar figures are an artifact of its 5,960 notional against `pdhl_sweep`'s
   13,280. Scaling `whale_absorb` up to the same notional leaves its PF at 1.32.
   **The lever is the size of the captured move, never the size of the position.**

4. **`range_fader` and `sweep_reclaim` are not literally coin flips** — they
   carry a small real raw edge (0.059%, 0.061%) *in the live record* that gross
   PF 1.00 concealed. **See the correction immediately below: for
   `sweep_reclaim` that edge does not survive out of sample.**
   This does **not** reopen their retirements; it closes them harder. At 0.37×
   the hurdle they would need to nearly *triple* the move they capture, which no
   sizing, frequency or leverage change can deliver. Jul 27's verdict was right;
   its stated reasoning was wrong in a way that mattered, because "coin flip"
   implied nothing was there, while "37% of the hurdle" says exactly how far
   away it is.

### CORRECTION to point 3, from this session's own screen — `sweep_reclaim`'s raw edge does not replicate

The screen landed `sweep_reclaim` on clean window-B tape after the above was
written, and its raw edge **flips sign**:

| **window B (clean)** | **94** | **−0.0607%** | **−0.38×** | **0.62** |

Same magnitude, opposite sign, on a **larger** sample. So the "small real raw
edge" I attributed to it is not a stable property — it is within the noise of
zero, and out of sample it is negative. The honest revision: **`sweep_reclaim`
is a coin flip after all, or worse**, and only `range_fader`'s +0.059% remains
as a live-only observation that has *not* been checked out of sample (its
window-B run did not finish this session).

`range_fader` was checked too, and it **does** replicate in sign, though barely:

| | n | edge yield | × hurdle | net PF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| live record | 108 | +0.0594% | +0.37× | 0.78 |
| **window B (clean)** | **114** | **+0.0274%** | **+0.17×** | **0.69** |

So `range_fader` really does carry a small positive raw edge on both records —
point 3 survives for it — but out of sample that edge is **halved, to 17% of the
hurdle.** It would need to capture nearly **six times** the move it does. That
is further from viable than the live number suggested, not closer.

This changes no status — both retirements become *more* secure, not less. The
net effect on point 3: **it applies to `range_fader` alone**, and the useful
lesson is about the framework rather than the strategies. Edge yield measures
what happened; a *small* edge yield on one window carries no more weight than
any other small number on one window, and `sweep_reclaim` is the proof.

### The gap, stated two ways — only one of them is honest about fragility

The book needs +0.047 points of edge yield (0.113% → 0.16%). By realized exit
geometry:

| exit reason | n | edge yield | avg hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| take-profit | 159 | **+1.416%** | 2.07h |
| time-stop | 10 | +1.589% | 12.00h |
| trend broke EMA50 | 29 | −0.346% | 1.18h |

Winners return 2.7× what losers cost, so the reward:risk geometry is not the

That framing is more useful and also more alarming: "30% short of costs" sounds

directions**. `vwap_revert_short` v1/v2 cleared the hurdle by 28% over 279

## Finding 3 — in the live book, stops too tight to clear costs are the dominant failure mode — but see Finding 4, it does NOT generalise

Finding 1 says the lever is the size of the captured move. This asks which
*ex-ante observable* controls it. The answer is the planned stop distance, and
the effect is large enough to account for essentially the whole book loss.

### First, the analysis I ran and then rejected — because a future session will otherwise run it and be fooled

Bucketing the 526 live trades by **hold time** gives a beautiful monotone result:
edge yield −0.263% under 0.5h rising to +1.078% beyond 4h, from −1.64× the
hurdle to +6.74×. It is **tautological and I am not using it**:

| hold bucket | n | % stopped out | % take-profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| <0.5h | 252 | **78.6%** | 15.9% |
| 0.5–1h | 80 | 65.0% | 30.0% |
| 1–2h | 76 | 46.1% | 48.7% |
| 2–4h | 64 | 34.4% | 56.3% |
| >4h | 54 | 38.9% | 40.7% |

Short holds *are* stop-outs — 78.6% of them. "Hold longer" is not an available
action: once the stop is hit the trade is over. Hold time is an **outcome**, not
a control. Anyone reading a hold-time table as advice will conclude "raise the
time-stop", which does nothing.

### The ex-ante version: bucket by the stop distance **planned at entry**

This one *is* a control — the strategy chooses it before the outcome exists:

| planned stop | n | avg stop | % TP | **edge yield** | **× hurdle** | net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **<0.5%** | **185** | 0.373% | 28.1% | **0.0250%** | **0.16×** | **−5,490** |
| **0.5–1%** | **253** | 0.674% | 33.6% | **0.2014%** | **1.26×** | **+1,404** |
| 1–2% | 70 | 1.379% | 20.0% | −0.0891% | −0.56× | −1,159 |
| 2–4% | 14 | 2.570% | 50.0% | 3.1396% | 19.62× | +1,363 |
| >4% | 4 | 4.514% | 25.0% | 2.7097% | 16.94× | +176 |

**The trades with stops under 0.5% lost 5,490 — more than the book's entire
−3,567 net loss.** They reach only 16% of the hurdle. The 0.5–1% band clears it
at 1.26× and is net positive.

### It is not Simpson's paradox — I checked, because this repo has been bitten by exactly that

Jul 27's Finding 2 was a pooled table that inverted on splitting. This one does
not. Within **every** strategy with adequate sample, the 0.5–1% band beats the
tight band, and the direction is unanimous:

| strategy | <0.5% | 0.5–1% |
|---|---|---|
| `vwap_revert_short` v1+v2 | n=94, yield 0.132% (0.83×), **net −565** | n=142, yield 0.242% (**1.51×**), **net +1,526** |
| `range_fader` | n=52, yield −0.072% (−0.45×), **net −2,622** | n=38, yield 0.345% (**2.16×**), **net +977** |
| `sweep_reclaim` | n=11, yield −0.309%, net −1,059 | n=20, yield 0.303%, net +433 |
| `vwap_revert_short` (all versions) | n=96, yield 0.124% | n=158, yield 0.209% |

Four independent strategies, same sign, pooled effect agreeing with every split.

**One caveat I owe this table, from later in the same session.**
`sweep_reclaim`'s *aggregate* live edge yield (+0.061%) did **not** replicate on
clean window-B tape (−0.061%, n=94 — see the correction under Finding 1). That
is a direct warning that live post-hoc splits of a strategy can be noise, and
`sweep_reclaim` is one of the four rows above. Its sub-samples are also the
smallest (n=11 and n=20). **Discount that row.** The finding does not rest on
it: `vwap_revert_short` v1/v2 (n=94 vs 142) and `range_fader` (n=52 vs 38) carry
the weight, and both are far larger. But a reader should not count "four
strategies" as four equally good pieces of evidence.

**And it reframes two retirements without reopening them.** `range_fader`'s
entire −1,770 is its tight-stop trades (−2,622); its 0.5–1% trades made +977 at
2.16× the hurdle. `sweep_reclaim` is the same shape. These were retired as
having "no edge"; the more accurate statement is that **they have an edge that
they systematically spend on trades whose geometry cannot pay.** That is not an
argument to un-retire them — n=38 and n=20 are below the judging floor, and the
selection is post-hoc — but it is a very different diagnosis, and it makes them
candidates for the cost-aware re-tune rather than dead ends.

### The relationship is NOT "wider is better" — it is a band, and the upper edge has a mechanism

1–2% stops are **negative** (−0.56× pooled; 0.01× within `vwap_revert_short`
v1/v2). The natural reading: at `tp_r` 1.5–2, a 1–2% stop puts the target 1.5–4%
away, and the engine's **12h time-stop** forecloses it. `pdhl_sweep`'s own
`max_sl_pct: 0.04` carries exactly that comment — *"2R unreachable in 12h"*.
So there is a floor set by costs and a ceiling set by the time-stop.

**The 2–4% and >4% buckets are not evidence.** n=14 and n=4, far below the n≥20
floor, and driven by 17 `vwap_revert_short` trades worth +1,398. A 19× hurdle
reading on 14 trades is noise, and I am not building on it.

### Why this matters more than the volatility story it partly subsumes

Stop distance is ATR-scaled, so a tight stop *is* a low-volatility entry. This is
therefore the **same phenomenon** as Jul 27's Finding 4 (the book reads +0.13 PF
better in high realized vol, unanimous across strategies) — seen through a much
better instrument:

- it is **per-trade**, not per-market-day;
- it is **observable at signal time**, so it can gate an entry;
- it has a **derived threshold** rather than a fitted one;
- and it is **already a parameter** in several strategies (`min_sl_pct`).

Critically, it **escapes the circularity Jul 27 used to refuse a vol gate.** That
report declined to test a volatility filter because "vol is precisely what
differs between windows A and B, so a vol gate passes A trivially and produces
near-zero trades in B". A stop-distance gate has no such problem: it filters
*within* a window, on a per-trade basis, so it is testable on a single window.
**That reopens the one research direction Jul 27 closed** — and it is the reason
the `pdhl_sweep` test below is worth running.

### The one thing this does NOT license

It does not license bolting `min_sl_pct` floors onto the book. Two reasons, both
of which apply even though the evidence above is strong:

- Raising the floor **discards trades**, and several strategies are already
  sample-starved. A filter that lifts PF while cutting n from 59 to 25 has not
  obviously improved anything.
- The band's **upper** edge is set by the 12h time-stop, which is a *global
  engine setting*, not a strategy parameter. The right fix might be to widen the
  time-stop rather than the stop distance, and this session has no evidence on
  which. Changing an engine-wide risk setting is not something to do
  unattended.

The one test run here is a single derived parameter on a single retired
strategy, with no status change. That is the appropriate size of step.

### Honesty about what this is

**This bucketing was not pre-registered.** It is post-hoc analysis of existing
trades and therefore hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory. Its confirmation
has to come from a prediction made in advance — which, by luck of ordering,
exists: commit `2a8f2ee` (15:16Z) derived a break-even stop distance of
**0.593%** for `pdhl_sweep` from geometry alone, and was pushed **before** this
table was computed. That independently-derived 0.593% falls **inside the
empirical transition** between the losing <0.5% band and the winning 0.5–1%
band. Two unrelated routes — algebra on `tp_r` and win rate, and bucketing live
outcomes — landing on the same threshold is the strongest single piece of
evidence this session produced.

## Finding 4 — THE PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTION FAILED, and the reason was outside all three readings I enumerated

`pdhl_sweep`, window B, `min_sl_pct` raised `0.004 → 0.006` exactly as
pre-registered in commit `2a8f2ee`. `.err` 0 bytes.

| | baseline (0.004) | **variant (0.006)** |
|---|---|---|
| trades | 59 | **38** |
| **net PF** | **1.07** | **0.88** |
| net | +233.55 | **−284.76** |
| win rate | 50.8% | **44.7%** |
| gross PF | 1.35 | 1.04 |
| avg hold | 1.67h | 1.93h |

**The prediction is falsified.** PF fell, it did not rise. n fell 59 → 38, a 36%
drop — far too large to be slot backfill, so the filter genuinely bit and the
comparison is a real filter effect rather than the reshuffle artifact I warned
about.

### The diagnostics say the payoff model was right where I doubted it and wrong where I didn't

I added the per-trade dump specifically to separate reading 2 ("payoff model was
optimistic") from reading 3 ("win rate depends on stop distance"). The answer is
neither:

| measured | value | model assumed |
|---|---|---|
| winner R-multiple | **+1.414R** | +1.500R |
| loser R-multiple | **−0.977R** | −1.000R |
| **winner stop width** | **1.0506%** | *(implicitly the same as losers)* |
| **loser stop width** | **1.3364%** | *(implicitly the same as winners)* |

**The R-multiples were essentially correct.** Reading 2 is refuted — the
optimism I corrected for before the run turned out not to be the problem.

**The fatal assumption was one I never wrote down: that stop width is
independent of outcome.** It is not. **Losers carry stops 27.2% wider than
winners.** Feeding the measured values back in reproduces the result exactly:

```
0.4474 × (+1.486%) + 0.5526 × (−1.3062%) = −0.0571%   (backtester: −0.057%)
```

whereas the equal-width model predicts **+0.1431%**. **That single assumption is
worth 0.200 percentage points of edge yield — more than the entire 0.16%
hurdle.** The correlation between stop width and losing is a bigger effect than
all transaction costs combined.

### What this does to Finding 3 — a real qualification, not a footnote

Finding 3's live table says trades with stops under 0.5% lose and 0.5–1% wins.
For `pdhl_sweep` on window B, raising the floor made things **worse**, and the
win rate fell from 50.8% to 44.7%.

**I initially wrote that this proves its tight-stop trades were its profitable
ones — computing "+518 of net over the 21 removed trades". That inference is
invalid and I have removed it.** It treats the variant's trade set as
baseline-minus-21, which is exactly the assumption my own pre-registration
warned against two paragraphs earlier: freed slots let *later* signals in, so
part of the −284.76 comes from trades the baseline never took. The n-drop to 38
shows net removal exceeded backfill; it does **not** license attributing the
whole delta to the removed band. See the direct measurement below, which answers
it without the set-difference.

The two results are reconcilable, and the reconciliation is testable rather than
convenient: raising a *floor* cannot move trades into the winning 0.5–1% band —
it can only delete trades below the floor. The surviving set has a mean stop of
**1.21%** and its deciles run 0.63 / 0.67 / 0.78 / 0.80 / 0.84 / 0.86 / 0.89 /
1.01 / 1.11 / 1.19 / 1.40 / 2.15 / 3.10 — i.e. **roughly half the surviving
trades sit above 1.0%, inside the band Finding 3 identifies as losing.** So the
floor pushed the distribution *through* the good band into the bad one.

If that reading is right, the lever is a **band-pass** (raise `min_sl_pct` *and*
lower `max_sl_pct` from its current 0.04) rather than a floor. **I am not
claiming that — it is a post-hoc explanation of a failed prediction, which is
exactly the kind of story this repo has been fooled by before.** It is written
here as the next pre-registerable test, not as a result.

### Finding 4b — I ran the follow-up in the same session, and the correlation is **not** universal

The obvious next question was whether the stop-width/outcome correlation is a
general law or a `pdhl_sweep` quirk. It needs no backtest — the live `positions`
ledger carries `entry_price` and `sl` for every trade — so it was answered
immediately:

| strategy | n | stop on winners | stop on losers | **losers wider by** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ema_trend_rider` | 42 | 0.5380% | 0.6514% | **+21.1%** |
| `pdhl_sweep` | 27 | 0.9449% | 1.0798% | **+14.3%** |
| `vwap_revert_short` | 304 | 0.7665% | 0.7943% | +3.6% |
| `range_fader` | 108 | 0.6747% | 0.6182% | **−8.4%** |
| `sweep_reclaim` | 35 | 0.7040% | 0.6213% | **−11.7%** |
| **pooled (all 526)** | **526** | **0.7417%** | **0.7415%** | **0.0%** |

**Pooled across the whole book the effect is exactly zero**, and per strategy it
runs in *both* directions. So the mechanism that broke the prediction is a
property of `pdhl_sweep` (and `ema_trend_rider`), **not** a law of the book.

Two things worth keeping:

- **It is a real property of `pdhl_sweep`, not noise.** Live gives +14.3%
  (n=27) and the window-B backtest gives +27.2% (n=38) — two independent
  samples, same direction. That is why the floor backfired on this strategy
  specifically.
- **It kills the speculation I was about to put on the watchlist.** I had
  written that if the correlation were universal it would mean ATR-scaled stop
  width is *anti*-selective — "a bigger finding than anything this session
  produced". It is not universal, so that theory is dead before anyone spends a
  session on it. **Answering it cost one query; chasing it would have cost a
  session.**

### Finding 4c — `0.008` too, and the degradation is strictly monotone

`0.008` was pre-registered as **information only, never a decision input**, and
it is used here only to establish the *shape* of the failure, not to select
anything. No status changed on any of it.

| `min_sl_pct` | n | net PF | net | win rate | **edge yield** | **× hurdle** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0.004** (shipped) | 59 | **1.07** | +234 | 50.8% | **+0.1898%** | **+1.19×** |
| 0.006 (derived) | 38 | 0.88 | −285 | 44.7% | +0.0850% | +0.53× |
| 0.008 (info only) | 27 | 0.77 | −400 | 40.7% | −0.0200% | −0.125× |

**Every column is monotone in the floor**, and monotone the wrong way. Trade
count, PF, net, win rate and edge yield all decline as the floor rises. That is
much stronger than a single failed point: it rules out "0.006 happened to be an
unlucky value" and says the direction itself is wrong for this strategy. The
threshold rule holds on both variants (0.53× → PF 0.88; −0.125× → PF 0.77), so
this is **20 of 20**.

#### CORRECTION — I misreported the variant's edge yield one commit ago

I wrote that v006's edge yield was **−0.057%, i.e. −0.36× hurdle**. That number
is the **unweighted** mean per-trade move from my payoff dump. **Edge yield is
notional-weighted**, and correctly computed it is **+0.0850% (+0.53×)** — a
different number and a different sign.

The two diverge because large-notional trades did relatively better than small
ones, and small-notional trades are by construction the *wide-stop* ones. The
threshold conclusion is unaffected (both are below 1×, PF is below 1 either
way), and Finding 4's payoff decomposition is unaffected because that
arithmetic is internally consistent in unweighted terms throughout. But the
figure quoted was the wrong statistic and would not have reconciled against the
screen table, where every other row is notional-weighted.

### What survives, and what I am withdrawing

- **Survives:** Finding 1 (edge yield, the hurdle, the threshold theorem) is
  untouched — see the corrected numbers in Finding 4c; the threshold rule
  holds on both variants.
- **Withdrawn:** the claim implicit in the addendum that `pdhl_sweep`'s floor was
  "set roughly a third too low". On the only clean window available, raising it
  made things worse. The author's 0.4% was better than my derived 0.6%.
- **Qualified:** Finding 3's direction is **not universal across strategies**.
  It is a pooled live result dominated by `vwap_revert_short` and `range_fader`;
  `pdhl_sweep` runs the other way. Anyone applying a stop-distance gate must
  test it **per strategy**, and this session is direct evidence that the pooled
  result does not transfer.
- **Reinforced, unexpectedly:** the geometry framing was productive even though
  the prediction was wrong. It generated a falsifiable claim, the claim was
  cleanly killed in one run, and the diagnostics identified a *new* mechanism
  (stop-width/outcome correlation) that is larger than the cost hurdle and that
  nobody in this repo had looked at. That is a better outcome than a vague
  confirmation would have been.
