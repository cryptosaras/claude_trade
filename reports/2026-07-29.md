## Finding 2 — why window B flattered it: the "edge" is nine singletons

The pre-registration allowed one diagnostic — distinct symbols and trades/day —
specifically to test whether A and B are two draws from one population. They are
not, and the answer turned out to be sharper than the question.

| | window A | window B |
|---|---|---|
| trades | 144 | 45 |
| **distinct symbols** | **28** | **14** |
| **trades/day** | **9.0** | **3.2** |

`pump_fade`'s entry needs a ≥4%/1h run, so its firing rate tracks how pumpy the
tape is; window A is roughly **3× pumpier**. But the decisive number is the
per-symbol split of window B's +548.90:

| symbol | n | net |
|---|---|---|
| `SYN_USDT` | **20** | **−183.58** |
| `TLM_USDT` | 6 | +138.34 |
| `SHIB_USDT` | 5 | +16.91 |
| `PEPE_USDT` | 3 | +191.86 |
| `GRASS_USDT` | 2 | +61.88 |
| **9 symbols with n=1 each** | **9** | **+323.49** |

- **59% of the profit comes from nine symbols that traded exactly once.** Those
  are single draws, not evidence of a repeatable pattern.
- **The one symbol with a real sample lost money.** `SYN_USDT` supplies 44% of
  the trades and −183.58 of PnL.

So the honest reading of window B is not "a clean out-of-sample win" but "a
14-symbol sample whose profit is concentrated in unrepeated events". Jul 28
called n=45 "nearly double the sample" of `whale_absorb`'s 26 — by trade count
that is true, by *independent bets* it is not.

**This is now a standing check** — trade count overstates sample size whenever a
strategy fires on rare events, so per-symbol concentration should be inspected
before any backtest edge is believed.

**And it discriminates — it does not simply flag everything.** The same
measurement on `pdhl_sweep`'s window B (n=59, PF 1.07, +233.55) reads the
opposite way:

| | `pump_fade` window B | `pdhl_sweep` window B |
|---|---|---|
| trades / symbols | 45 / **14** | 59 / **25** |
| trades per day | 3.2 | 4.2 |
| symbols with n=1 | 9 | 7 |
| **net from the n=1 symbols** | **+323.49 (59% of profit)** | **−134.73 (a drag)** |
| **net from multi-trade symbols** | +225.41 | **+368.28** |

`pdhl_sweep`'s profit comes from symbols it traded repeatedly and its singletons
*lose*; `pump_fade`'s profit is the singletons. That is the difference between a
pattern and a lucky draw, and it is invisible in `n`, PF, or edge yield.

### The bucket shape differs sharply between windows — and the confound is in-sample vs out-of-sample

The baseline's own window-A trades, bucketed by planned stop distance, against
Jul 28's Finding 4d table for the *same strategy* on window B:

| band | **window A (today)** | window B (Jul 28) |
|---|---|---|
| ~0.4–0.6% | +1.02× | +1.59× |
| 0.6–0.8% | **+0.03×** | **+2.08× (peak)** |
| 0.8–1.0% | **+4.19×** | +1.20× |
| 1.0–1.5% | **+3.29×** | **−0.54×** |
| >1.5% | **+3.43×** | **−4.17×** |

**The upper bands invert completely.** Window B's worst band is window A's best;
window B's peak is window A's worst. On window A the trades above 1.0% carry
**+886 of the +955 net** — so capping at 0.010 deleted almost the entire profit,
which is precisely what happened.

**The window-B column was re-run today with the same driver** to make this
apples-to-apples rather than a comparison against yesterday's numbers on
since-changed data: **n=59, PF 1.07, net +233.55**, and the buckets reproduce
Jul 28's Finding 4d **to the digit** (+1.59 / +2.08 / +1.20 / −0.54 / −4.17).
So the difference between the columns is not driver drift and not data drift.

**But it is confounded, and I cannot separate the two explanations:**

1. **In-sample vs out-of-sample** (leading). Window A is `pdhl_sweep`'s tuning
   window, so its bucket table partly reflects which bands the tuning favoured,
   not an independent measurement of stop-distance profitability.
2. **Noise** (secondary). Buckets hold **n=7–18 on both sides**, all below the

Jul 28's Finding 4d carried its own n=10/n=7 caveat, and that caveat was
under-weighted when item 3 was ranked as the "measured next step". **The
operative rule survives either explanation: do not derive a parameter from a
per-bucket table whose buckets hold n<20.**

### The line should stop — but for the right reason

| change | window | in/out of sample | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `min_sl_pct` 0.004 → 0.006 → 0.008 | B | **out** | PF 1.07 → **0.88** → **0.77** |
| `max_sl_pct` 0.040 → 0.010 | A | **in (tuning)** | PF 1.27 → **1.02** |

Only the first row is a genuine out-of-sample falsification. The second is the

and the bucket shape motivating the other direction does not survive contact
with a second window. **Stop-distance re-tuning of `pdhl_sweep` is a dead end.**

It does not overturn the *cost* framework — the 0.16% hurdle and the

refuted is the idea that *stop distance* is the dial that moves edge yield.
