## PRE-REGISTRATION (written and committed BEFORE any window-A number was known)

The failure mode this guards against is not a bad backtest — it is a sixth
report that changes nothing while the book sits empty. And the opposite failure:
lowering the bar afterwards to manufacture an action. So the rule is fixed here.

### Why two windows, not the June screen the last report proposed

Watchlist item 1 said "screen the book on the clean Jun 9–25 window". Run alone,
that reproduces exactly the ambiguity v3 left behind — *PF 1.29 on June, 0.62 on
July, now what?* — once per strategy. A single window cannot separate a real edge
from tape that happened to suit it. Two windows three weeks apart can, and it
costs one extra run **per survivor**, not per strategy.

| | window | span | contamination |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | `--days 16 --end 2026-06-25T00:00` | Jun 9 → Jun 25 | **clean** — all live tuning data begins Jul 5; nothing in the book has seen June |
| **B** | `--days 14 --end 2026-07-27T00:00` | Jul 13 → Jul 27 | **forward** of the last file edit for 12 of 16 strategies |

Window B overlap to declare honestly: `range_fader` (edited Jul 14),
`pdhl_sweep` (Jul 17), `vwap_revert_short_capped` (Jul 18) and
`vwap_revert_short` (Jul 18 floor / Jul 21 status) had file edits *inside*
window B. For those four, B is partly in-sample and their B number is weaker
evidence than the other twelve's. Every file edit in the book is on or before
Jul 21, so B is fully out-of-sample for the remaining twelve. The book has been
completely dormant since Jul 21, so no window-B tape was influenced by live
trading.

### Triage and action thresholds

1. **Window A is run single-shot (untwinned) as a triage pass only.** Pinned
   `--end` makes a single run reliable enough to filter on; no single-shot number
   is quoted as a decision input.
2. **First filter is n, not PF.** Nine of the sixteen never traded live at all.
   If a strategy cannot produce **n ≥ 20** in 16 days of replay it is unusable
   whatever its PF, and that is the cheapest discriminator available.
3. Advance to window B only if **n_A ≥ 20 AND PF_A ≥ 1.10**.
4. **Status change to `incubating` requires all four, with twin runs
   byte-identical on both windows:** n_A ≥ 20, PF_A ≥ 1.10, n_B ≥ 20, PF_B ≥ 1.10.
   Sub-threshold on either window ⇒ stays `retired`, and the number is reported.
5. **`vwap_revert_short` is exempt and stays `paused` regardless of what it
   prints.** It is under the Jul 21 pre-registered human gate. Its numbers are
   informational only.
6. **At most 3 promotions this session.** If more qualify, take the top 3 by
   `min(PF_A, PF_B)`. Rationale: CLAUDE.md caps the book at ~8 running
   strategies, and re-seeding the whole book off one screen is churn.
7. Twin pairs are run **back-to-back**, not split across the session. The events
   log shows `funding history backfilled for 1 symbols` four times on Jul 26
   alone — that is the documented mechanism by which a pinned window legitimately
   changes between runs, and splitting a pair invites misreading a real data
   change as nondeterminism.

**If nothing clears the bar, the report says nothing cleared.** That is a valid
outcome and the threshold does not move.

## Finding 1 — the June/July split IS separable ex ante, and the separator is
## realized volatility, not ADX

This is watchlist item 2 from Jul 26, answered. The question was: *v3 makes
PF 1.29 on June tape and 0.5–0.62 on July tape — is the difference visible in
advance?* Computed from BTC 1h candles with the repo's own `indicators.adx`:

| | window A (Jun 9–25) | window B (Jul 13–27) |
|---|---|---|
| BTC ADX mean | **25.5** | **25.3** |
| BTC ADX p90 | 35.1 | 35.2 |
| **realized vol (24h σ of 1h returns)** | **0.44%** | **0.29%** |
| **BTC hi–lo range over window** | **15.8%** | **8.3%** |
| BTC direction | −4.4% | +2.3% |

**ADX does not separate the two windows at all** — 25.5 vs 25.3, and even the
p90 tails match to within 0.1. **Volatility separates them cleanly**: June's
tape was 52% more volatile and had nearly double the range. The weekly series
shows July as a monotonic volatility compression:

| week ending | BTC ADX | realized vol | BTC chg |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 14 | 24.5 | 0.42% | +3.8% |
| Jun 21 | 27.1 | 0.41% | −3.7% |
| Jun 28 | 21.3 | 0.58% | −5.9% |
| Jul 5 | 31.1 | 0.37% | +6.8% |
| Jul 12 | 22.3 | 0.38% | +0.2% |

| Jul 26 | 25.9 | 0.28% | +1.1% |
| **current** | **34.9** | **0.23%** | +0.2% |

That is a coherent mechanism, not a curve-fit: a stretch-fade strategy needs
volatility to *produce* the stretch and to *pay* for the snap-back against

**Two consequences, stated before the screen results are known:**

   and it is still falling. Today is the *worst* observed condition for the

2. **Prediction, recorded before window B runs:** the screen should read
   systematically weaker on window B than window A for stretch/fade strategies,
   and the gap should be largest for the ones with the widest entry stretch
   requirement. If window B instead reads *stronger* across the board, this
   volatility story is wrong and I should say so.

## v3's failure

Having found ADX 35.8 labelled SIDE, the obvious hypothesis was that SIDE-gated
strategies are being fed trending tape. Tested against **live** trades (the
strongest evidence class in this repo) by joining each position's `entry_ts` to
the newest `regime.meta->>'adx'` at or before it:

|---|---|---|---|---|

| SIDE | <25 | 339 | −1426 | **0.94** |

Read naively that says high-ADX SIDE is the *better* tape — the opposite of the
hypothesis. **It is a Simpson's-paradox artifact.** Split per strategy:

| strategy | ADX bucket | n | net | PF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `range_fader` | ≥25 | **12** | +985 | 2.85 |
| `range_fader` | <25 | 95 | −2685 | 0.64 |

The pooled row's positive sign comes almost entirely from **12 `range_fader`
trades** (+985) outweighing v3's −314. n=12 is far below the n≥20 floor and is
not evidence of anything. For the only SIDE strategy with real sample size the
split runs the predicted way: **1.12 at ADX<25 (n=223) vs 0.91 at ADX≥25
(n=51)**. Modest, directionally right, ex-ante observable.

But splitting by version era — the Jul 26 lesson applied — kills it as an
explanation of v3:

| era | ADX bucket | n | net | PF | ADX range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1+v2 (before Jul 18 06:16Z) | <25 | 201 | +2290 | **1.18** | 10–25 |

**v3 lost in low-ADX SIDE — the exact tape where v1/v2 made PF 1.18.** Only 3

I published the bolded claim above ("version delta, not a tape delta… textbook

|---|---|---|---|---|

Arithmetic checks exactly: `4341.10 − 7921.68 + 13.43 = −3567.15`. (The
`funding_paid` column is *negative* across the book, i.e. funding was collected
on net — confirming the Jul 26 finding that the funding item is immaterial, and
in the favourable direction.)

**Stated carefully, because the loose version invites the one move CLAUDE.md
forbids.** Per trade: gross edge **$8.29** against fees of **$15.12**. On
$15,125 average notional that gross edge is **0.055% of notional**. Slippage
(0.03%/side = 0.06% round trip) is *already inside* `gross_pnl` via the
entry/exit prices, so the raw signal is worth about **0.115% of notional**
against a total round-trip cost of about **0.16%**.

So the honest headline is **not** "fees spoiled a winning book". It is: **the
signal generates roughly 70% of its own toll.** The book is directionally right
more often than not — gross PF 1.14 is a real, non-random edge — but the edge is
about a third short of the size it needs to survive contact with realistic
costs. Closing a 30% gap is a plausible research target; it is not a rounding
error, and it is emphatically not an argument for touching `fee_taker` or
`slippage`, which stay exactly where they are.

### Verification: the n=0/n=1 screen results are real, not silent crashes

Worth checking before treating a low trade count as "no edge". `core.step` wraps
every `should_enter`/`should_exit` call in `try/except` and reports via
`emit`, which the backtester **does not pass** (`emit = on_event or (lambda
kind, msg: None)` — `app/engine/core.py:71`, and the call site at
`app/backtest/main.py:153` supplies no `on_event`). So a strategy throwing on
every tick emits nothing and returns valid JSON with `trades: 0`. A crashed
strategy and a dead one would be indistinguishable — and my first-pass filter
is n.

`log.exception` still fires, and I checked empirically rather than assuming:
reproducing that exact call in the container writes **131 bytes to stderr**
(Python's `lastResort` handler, since no `basicConfig` runs in the backtest
path). Every screen run's `.err` file is **0 bytes**, so no strategy crashed.
The low trade counts are genuine.

### Why the "cost share of the move" framing I reached for first is wrong

Worth recording because it nearly became the headline. Current tape's median
2h move across the 74 group symbols is 0.44% against 0.16% round-trip cost, and
at the book's median 0.54h hold the median move is only ~0.23% — cost is 70% of
it. That looked damning. **It is the wrong denominator.** Actual exits do not
land on the median move:

| exit reason | n | share | avg hold | avg realized move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stop-loss | 326 | 62.2% | 0.93h | 0.77% |
| take-profit | 159 | 30.3% | 2.07h | 2.00% |
| trend broke EMA50 | 29 | 5.5% | 1.18h | 0.42% |
| time-stop | 10 | 1.9% | 12.00h | 2.02% |

SL/TP levels are ATR-scaled, so realized moves are 0.77–2.00% and cost is
8–20% of them, not 70%. The problem is **not** that trades are too small to
clear the spread. It is that the win/loss *mix* leaves only $8.29 of edge per
trade against a $15.12 toll. Same conclusion, correct mechanism — and only the
correct mechanism tells you the fix is edge per trade, not hold length.

### What this implies for the search (not acted on this session)

The lever is **edge per trade**, and fees are proportional to notional, so
trading *less often with more selectivity* is the only direction that helps.
Raising the entry bar is exactly what v3's 4.5-ATR floor attempted — and note
it did raise gross quality on the tape it was tuned on. This does not mean the
floor was right; it means the *category* of fix is right and the book has never
been tuned with a cost-aware objective. Every parameter in `strategies/` was
chosen to maximise PF or win rate. **None was chosen to maximise net edge per
trade against a $15 toll.** That is the most promising unexplored direction in
this repo, and it needs a human-in-the-loop session because it implies
re-tuning strategies currently marked retired.

## Finding 6 — CORRECTION TO MY OWN PRE-REGISTRATION: window A is **in-sample** for most of the book, not clean held-out

The pre-registration at the top of this report calls window A (Jun 9–25)
"clean — nothing in the book has seen June". **That is wrong, and the error is
inherited from the Jul 26 report's Finding 4.** That finding established that
all *live* tuning data begins Jul 5 and concluded June was therefore untouched.
It overlooked how the strategies were originally authored: not from live
results, but from **event studies and backtests run at creation time**, over
the preceding 21–30 days.

Most of the book was created **Jul 5–6**. A 30-day study run on Jul 6 spans
roughly **Jun 6 → Jul 6 — which fully contains window A.** `pdhl_sweep`'s own
docstring says so explicitly: *"30d PF 1.31 (n=144)… filter/group/regimes were
all chosen by maximizing over the same 30d, so 1.31 is selection-inflated."*
Its parameters were frozen 2026-07-06 and the only later commit (Jul 17)
changed `status: incubating → retired` and nothing else — verified by diff.

**So the window labels invert for most of the book:**

| window | what it actually is |
|---|---|
| A — Jun 9–25 | **in-sample** for strategies authored Jul 5–6 (most of the book) |
| B — Jul 13–27 | **genuinely out-of-sample** — after every parameter freeze; the only later edits are status flips, which a forced-active `--strategy NAME` run ignores |

This does not weaken the screen — **it strengthens what the screen already
shows, and redirects which number decides.** Two consequences:

1. **Every strategy except one failed on its own tuning window.** Printing
   ≤1.01 on the very data your parameters were selected against is a far
   harsher verdict than failing out-of-sample. Selection bias should flatter
   these numbers, and it didn't.
2. **Window B is the decisive test, not the confirmation.** I had it backwards.
   The pre-registered thresholds are unchanged — n≥20 and PF≥1.10 on both
   windows — but the *meaning* is: window A is now a sanity check that a
   strategy can at least reproduce its own tuning, and window B carries the
   evidence.

I am **not** relaxing the rule to compensate. A strategy that cannot clear 1.10
in-sample has no case, so the gate still filters correctly; it is just filtering
on a weaker signal than I claimed when I wrote it.

### `pdhl_sweep` — the one survivor, and why its window A number was expected

Its own docstring pre-registered this outcome in July: in-sample 30d PF 1.31,
*"true edge is likely ~1.1–1.2"*, with instructions to *"judge on FORWARD live
PF at ≥20 trades ONLY (do not anchor on 1.31); kill on forward PF < 1.15."*

That forward test already ran and it failed: **live n=27, net PF 0.85** (gross
PF 1.05 — so a sliver of gross edge, fully consumed by costs), and it was
retired Jul 17 under that pre-registered rule.

So window A's **1.27 is in-sample and almost exactly what the strategy's author
predicted in-sample.** It is not new evidence. **Window B is** — it begins
Jul 13, a week after the Jul 6 parameter freeze, and no parameter has changed
since. Twin pairs on both windows are running now, back-to-back per
pre-registration point 7.

### `whale_absorb` — the one strategy in this book that was never falsified

Different case entirely, and the more interesting one. Created **2026-07-06 as
`paused, pre-gate`** — its parameters were declared from a 30-day event study
(so window A is in-sample for it too) and then **it was never activated.** It
has **zero live trades**, has never been through the validation gate, and has
sat untouched for three weeks. Every other retirement in this book rests on
live evidence; this one was never tested at all.

Its docstring is unusually disciplined about its own weaknesses — it records
that a tight stop destroys the edge (PF 0.58) and that only a wide 2.5-ATR stop
survives, that the mirror short side was flat and was killed, and that SIDE
"died in the recent half" so the strategy declares **BULL+BEAR only**.

That last choice is what makes window B a real test: window B is **38% BULL /
13% BEAR**, and `whale_absorb`'s BULL sample in window A is **n=10 at PF 0.97**.
Its entire window-A result is the BEAR half. If the edge is genuinely
BEAR-specific, window B should read close to 1.0, and it will not clear the
gate — correctly, because a strategy that needs a regime the market is not
currently in cannot be incubated to gate standard now.

Two things worth keeping, though, because they are more interesting than the
verdict:

1. **The BEAR concentration did not carry, and that is reassuring rather than

   signal wearing a ~$13 toll. It is the single best candidate in the book for
   the cost-aware re-tuning direction described in Finding 5 — and that work
   needs a human, because it means re-tuning a strategy currently marked
   retired.

*pessimistic* by a similar margin. Both bracket the truth.
